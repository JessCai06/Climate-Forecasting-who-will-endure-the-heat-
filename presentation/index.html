    Historical Data: Temperature

    Trends

    • Cooling degree days: +19% in the median city, 1976–2005 to the 2019–2025 forecast; up in 193 of 197 cities.
    • Days with a high of 95 °F or more: +21% across all cities.
    • Nights with a low of 75 °F or more: +48%. Nights warm faster than days.

    Findings

    • The rise lands where it is already hot: Texas, the Gulf Coast, the Southwest.
    • Our forecast ran low against what happened (rank correlation 0.97 across cities), so these numbers are conservative.

    Energy use

    Findings: each 1 °F hotter day adds about 2.2% to U.S. peak demand (1.2–3.4% by region); a 1 °F warmer night lifts the overnight floor about 1.5%.

    Energy Affordability

    Findings: hot years did not raise prices (2002–2018); a low-income single person was over the bar in 45 of 46 states, a family of four in 36.

    What these three datasets tell us: weather, energy use, and energy affordability

    Findings: heat moves use and peaks, not prices; and bills were already over the affordable line before the heat arrived.
